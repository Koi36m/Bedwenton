## 2. New design hypothesis

The owner believes the reference video's large rotating white shape may be formed by **two directional END_ROD particle streams**, rather than by separately plotting an outer circle and its internal curves.

Test that hypothesis directly.

**Emitter A:** Spawn exactly one END_ROD particle with an initially upward-pointing velocity.

**Emitter B:** Spawn exactly one END_ROD particle with an initially downward-pointing velocity.

Both emitters originate at or very near the same point behind the player's torso.

Their emission directions rotate together over time while staying 180 degrees apart.

Do not interpret this as two emitters spinning in opposite angular directions.

## 3. Mathematics

Let `age` be the number of game ticks since activation.

Initial rotation period: 80 ticks.

Use the vertical plane behind the player's body.

Define:

`theta = PI/2 + 2*PI*(age % 80)/80`

`directionA = (cos(theta), sin(theta), 0)`

`directionB = -directionA`

These are player-local unit directions.

Transform both directions into world space using the current player body yaw.

Do not rotate directions using head yaw or camera pitch.

The emitters share a stationary player-relative origin:

- Center height: approximately feet +1.55 blocks.
- Backward offset: approximately 1.0 block.
- Local vertical XY plane.

Both new particle directions must change with the animation phase.

The mathematical period should continue advancing on ticks without emissions.

## 4. Directional END_ROD spawning

Paper documents directional emission using:

- `Particle.END_ROD`
- `count = 0`
- offset X/Y/Z as the direction
- nonzero `extra` as the speed multiplier

For every emission step, send one particle using direction A and one using direction B.

Begin with a conservative speed around 0.15, subject to actual Paper 26.3 behavior.

Test three bounded speed presets if useful:

- Slow: 0.10
- Normal: 0.15
- Fast: 0.22

These are experimental starting values, not guaranteed final physical velocities.

Initial cadence: one opposing pair every 4 ticks.

Do not emit an array of stationary geometry points.

Do not pre-create a circle or any Bézier strokes.

Do not add head, ground, crown or ambient particles to this experiment.

The objective is to determine whether the two moving streams naturally form a visually readable rotating shape.
