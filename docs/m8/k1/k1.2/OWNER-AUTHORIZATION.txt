2. Corrected creative direction

IMPORTANT: The owner has supplied a hand-drawn image. Treat it as the primary visual reference for this correction.

Previous implementations misunderstood the intended placement.

K1 featured a horizontal waist circle.

K1.1 featured a horizontal crescent behind the waist.

Neither represents the requested effect.

The intended effect is a vertical magical circle floating behind the player's back, with curved internal strokes that visibly rotate inside the circle.

The drawing shows:

Front/back relationship: a circular magical formation located behind the player's torso.

Side profile: the formation floats behind the character rather than surrounding their waist.

Ring design: a complete outer circumference containing an asymmetric, curved, flowing internal pattern that rotates.

Implement this design, not another horizontal waist effect.

3. Geometry and placement

Outer circle

Particle: white END_ROD.

Shape: complete circular outline.

Orientation: vertical plane, not horizontal XZ ground/waist plane.

Center: behind the player's torso/back.

Suggested initial radius: approximately 0.75–0.80 blocks.

Suggested initial center height: 1.25 blocks above feet.

Suggested initial backward offset: 0.65 blocks.

Follow player body yaw.

Maintain the same relative placement when the player walks, jumps, turns, teleports, or changes dimensions.

Coordinate requirements:

The existing player-local frame uses:

+X = player's left.

+Y = up.

+Z = forward.

−Z = behind the player.

Therefore the vertical ring should lie in the local XY plane, with its center translated along negative local Z.

A representative local circle is:

(radius * cos(angle), centerHeight + radius * sin(angle), -backOffset)

Evaluate the scene using the existing M1 Pose/Quat body-yaw anchor and M5 composition APIs.

Never use fixed world directions for the backward offset.

These dimensions are initial design targets. Minor adjustments are permitted when necessary for geometry or visual clarity, but document the actual values.

Appearance

The outer circumference should be:

bright but elegant;

clearly circular;

visually separate from the player;

readable from behind and at an angle;

not unnecessarily dense.

Do not create a particle-filled disc.

4. Rotating internal design

This is the most important new visual requirement.

The owner drew curved internal lines inside the circle to indicate visible rotation.

Implement a minimalist, asymmetric design consisting of two gently curved sweeping strokes, together resembling a flowing S-shaped or paired arcane swirl.

The strokes must:

lie inside the vertical outer ring;

use white END_ROD particles;

resemble smooth curved lines rather than straight spokes;

remain contained within the outer circumference;

rotate continuously around the center of the vertical disc;

remain visible from behind the character;

avoid forming a thick particle cloud.

Use the existing M3 curve, M4 sampling and M5 motion systems wherever appropriate.

Rotation requirements

The internal design must rotate in the plane of the circle, about the circle's local normal axis.

It must not merely oscillate left to right.

It must not swing the entire circle in front of the player.

It must not rotate the disc edge-on to the player.

Target one complete revolution approximately every 60–100 ticks, using deterministic M2 timing.

Choose and document the initial period.

The asymmetry of the inner curved strokes must make the rotation visually obvious.

A uniformly spaced circular outline alone is insufficient: a symmetrical circle can mathematically rotate without providing a visible rotational cue.

Keep the outer circumference stable or subtly animated while the inner strokes rotate.
